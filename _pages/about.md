I am Bin Huang, a Research Associate at Case Western Reserve University working on machine learning for medical image analysis. My research aims to make AI models trustworthy enough for real clinical use: models that generalize across imaging devices and institutions, quantify their own uncertainty, and offer interpretable decisions.

My work spans both medical image analysis and imaging itself. On the analysis side, I work across ultrasound, CT, MRI, and PET, covering segmentation, registration, classification, and image synthesis, with a recent focus on uncertainty-aware adaptation of foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM). On the imaging side, I develop deep learning methods for magneto-acousto-electrical tomography (MAET), aiming at robust conductivity reconstruction from sparse measurements. I enjoy working closely with clinicians to turn methodological advances into tools for diagnosis and treatment.

I have published 29 peer-reviewed papers, 13 of them as first or co-first author, in leading journals and conferences such as IEEE TMI, IEEE JBHI, and MICCAI. See [Publications](/publications/) for the full list.

My research interests include:
* Model generalizability and interpretability, including uncertainty estimation for foundation models such as the Segment Anything Model (SAM);
* Magneto-acousto-electrical tomography (MAET) reconstruction and conductivity imaging;
* AI-assisted diagnosis and therapy for nasopharyngeal carcinoma and liver disease;
* Segmentation, classification, registration, and image generation for medical images.
